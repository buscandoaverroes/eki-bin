# eki-bin v1.7

_The firmware running on bottle-01, and everything since `main` last moved
(the initial commit, 2026-07-12): 18 merged PRs into `dev` plus
`feature/interaction-logger`, from July 13 to November 8, 2026._

## In one paragraph

In July, eki-bin was a MicroPython loop on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2W syncing
time over WiFi and driving an 8-LED stick. In November it's a bottle that
gets lived with. A radio-less XIAO RP2350 keeps time with a DS3231 and shows
each upcoming train as a point of light closing in on a "station" point,
one colour per line, behind thick brown glass. Tap wakes it and changes line,
tilt sets the brightness, and it says goodnight when no train is in reach.
Most of the work in between was finding out that the vessel, the room and
the hand are parameters of the design, not a container for it. And most of
the later work was building the means to *measure* that instead of tuning
by feel.

## The unit

| | July (`main`) | v1.7 |
|---|---|---|
| board | Raspberry Pi Pico 2W | Seeed XIAO RP2350 (no radio) |
| time | WiFi + NTP at boot | DS3231 RTC, survives power cycles, no network |
| light | 8-LED stick | 21-LED WS2812B strip inside a brown glass bottle |
| input | none | LSM6DSV16X IMU: tap and tilt |
| firmware | one `main.py`, 850 lines | `main.py` + 15 modules, no radio code on a radio-less board |
| host tests | 7 test files | 600+ tests, lint, run before every upload |

## What changed, by thread

### Hardware path: from a Pico on WiFi to a radio-less bottle

- **Qi wireless power bring-up and NFC research** (July). ST25DV bench
  results and a decision: provisioning by a first-party phone app, not
  Shortcuts, and a timetable doesn't fit on an NFC sticker anyway.
  [`docs/nfc-provisioning.md`](../nfc-provisioning.md)
- **System pinouts** ([#4](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/4)):
  alongside per-board pin maps, a file per *build* showing how its parts
  connect to each other.
- **DS3231 real-time clock** ([#7](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/7),
  [#8](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/8),
  [#9](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/9)): brought up on two
  boards, then made the time source. Verified the way that matters: unplug
  for a minute, reboot, correct time. About two thirds of the integration
  commits were latent bugs that this board surfaced.
- **V1.6, single target** ([#10](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/10)):
  `main.py` from 3,127 lines to 616, split into eleven modules with zero
  star-imports. Triggered by a hardware fact turned into a crash:
  `import network` at line 17 killed a radio-less board *before* `config.py`
  was read. `ruff` joined `make test`.
- **Provisioning** ([#11](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/11)):
  a unit is buildable by following a document. The runbook had drifted past
  out of date to actively wrong (it said to disable a working feature).
  Three silent states that looked like broken hardware are now visible.
  [`docs/provisioning-runbook.md`](../provisioning-runbook.md)

### Display: what the light says

- **`ApproachContract`** ([#1](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/1)):
  a train is a single light moving toward a fixed anchor, not an arc
  growing and shrinking. Bidirectional, several trains per arm, and a
  three-tier brightness model settled on real hardware.
- **Boot ceremony** ([#3](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/3)):
  a bounded, one-time "hanabi" burst; a persistent error colour on failure.
- **Colour is measured, not guessed** ([#12](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/12)):
  the low-PWM floor of the strip, and how many hues the glass actually
  separates (thick brown: 2–3). A marker-tick bug turned out to be
  arithmetic: the default landed on raw value 1 per channel, exactly where
  WS2812B channel matching collapses.
- **Day/night brightness and tilt-to-adjust** ([#13](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/13)):
  one brightness cannot serve a room that swings two to three orders of
  magnitude between noon and midnight. Tilt sets it by hand.
- **The motion vocabulary** ([#14](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/14)):
  five motion words bound to tap outcomes. `outward` on wake (scaled to how
  hard you tapped), `around` on a line change, `shake` when there's nothing
  to change to.
- **Palette checks at boot** ([#15](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/15)):
  the usable brightness window, reported, not fatal. It found a real problem
  in the live config on its first run.
- **Goodnight and the morning flower** ([#16](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/16)):
  ceremonies for "trains exist, but none is in reach".
- **The crawl sandbox** ([#17](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/17)):
  six ways to move a train, five station fades, compared on real glass.
  The smooth ones won every time. The ones that looked alive read as
  "something needs your attention", which is the opposite of ambient. That
  is now a design principle.

### Input: what the hand does

- **The gesture envelope** ([#5](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/5)):
  tap recognition with a two-phase ACK/CONFIRM response, and a deliberate
  scope reduction. Tap *position* and *count* didn't survive testing at
  scale: separability swung 96.7% → 71.2% → 81.7% between sessions. Tap
  stayed.
- **Gestures in the real loop** ([#6](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/6)):
  a tap cycles the **line** shown, not the station. The bottle sits in one
  room, so the station is fixed; the line is what varies.
- **The interaction plan** ([#18](https://github.com/buscandoaverroes/eki-bin/pull/18)):
  tap and tilt fight each other. That's a composition problem, not a
  classification one, so the plan was to record, replay, then arbitrate.
- **Record, replay, simulate, arbitrate** (`feature/interaction-logger`, the
  last branch in this release):
  - A raw 240 Hz recorder, and a **guided protocol** where the script cues
    each trial so both hands do the motion.
  - A **replay harness** that runs the real firmware code over recordings at
    any loop rate.
  - A **simulator** with detect → arbiter → classify as swappable blocks. With
    the shipped blocks it reproduces replay event for event, and it can run
    live beside a recording.
  - What it found: half of natural taps never reached the classifier. The
    trigger watched the *length* of the acceleration vector, and a sideways
    tap barely changes it. No fixed window separates "hold, then tilt" from
    "tap, wait, then grab", but stillness does.
  - With a vector trigger and an **arbiter** that commits a tap only once the
    bottle is proved still, in simulation: natural taps 15/30 → 28/30, grab
    trials with a false tap 16/30 → 6/30, no false taps in 70 minutes of
    rest. The arbiter ships in the firmware **switched off**; it goes on
    after more rest data and a session in the bottle.
  - On the bottle now: the IMU **resetting itself** (the "asleep and can't be
    woken" bug, traced by the recordings to the wiring) is detected and
    recovered from. A gyro check rejects handling, with a baseline that
    abstains until proven. Knocks that arrive through the table are
    rejected. Tilt timing no longer depends on the loop rate. Timestamps
    survive the 12.4-day `ticks_ms` wrap.
  - [`docs/imu-interaction-plan.md`](../imu-interaction-plan.md)

### Tooling and process

- `make test`: 7 test files in July, 600+ tests now, plus `ruff`. It runs
  before every upload and refuses to upload on a failure.
- `make doctor` (is this board healthy?), `make dev` (run from the Mac with
  zero flash writes), `make upload` with hash-verified copies.
- `make leakcheck` plus a pre-push hook: this is a public repo about a
  private commute, so real timetables, recordings and `config.py` are
  gitignored and scanned for.
- `make pr` (push and open a PR, confirmed and single-use),
  `make rtc-drift UNIT=…` (the clock's drift, per unit).
- `make imu-record`, `make replay`, `make sim`, `make sim-live`.

## Known limitations, stated plainly

- **Tap and tilt still sometimes fight on the bottle.** The fix works in
  simulation and isn't switched on yet.
- **The IMU resets itself occasionally**: 12 times in 11 minutes of handling,
  twice in 70 minutes at rest. The firmware recovers; the cure is a wiring
  harness.
- **A tilt can stay engaged** after the bottle is set down slightly leaning
  (failure C in the plan). Open.
- **Every input measurement is one bottle on one table.** Other vessels and
  rooms are the next validation, not an assumption.
